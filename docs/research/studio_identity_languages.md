# Studio identity languages: art-directed digital, motion and generative identities

Scope note: search tooling returned thin snippets and a few fetched pages. Only claims with a URL are "cited". Anything from my own background knowledge is in Inferences or Gaps and is unverified. Coverage is partial: many requested studios (Pentagram, Wolff Olins, Locomotive, ManvsMachine, Buck, Mother Design, Order, Spin, Ordinary Folk, Hello Monday, Bureau Borsche, Superunion) were NOT verified in depth.

## Which studios and projects are the best exemplars, and what does each language consist of?

### Takeaway
The best-documented exemplars split into (a) studios that ship a real generative tool (Field.io: IBM Quantum, Adidas, 113 Spring), (b) physics/concept-driven motion systems (Collins OFFF, Muse Group; Studio Dumbar/DEPT), and (c) in-house texture/treatment toolkits (Figma Config 2026: dithering, lo-fi effects).

### Cited Findings
- Figma Config 2026 (Figma Brand Studio, creative director Chelsea White). Three core textures: scribbly linework, blurred gradients, oval particles. Glyphs range "sketchy, to generative, to crisp". Tools built in Figma Make apply "lo-fi effects" to images. A dithering tool gave speaker headshots one consistent treatment (pointillist quality). Static and motion built in parallel (Relay); motion is "looser, more collage-y". Physical extension: extruded foam sculptures. — [Figma Blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/the-visual-identity-behind-config-2026/)
- IBM Quantum (Field.io). Brand visuals driven by three parameters drawn from physics: superposition, entanglement, interference, exposed as interactive parameters in a "generative brand tool" that makes "an infinite array" of assets. At the summit, a generative "dawn" evolved through the day and per-attendee outputs from location data were printed on badges and invites. — [Field.io](https://field.io/work/ibm-quantum-brand-visuals)
- Adidas REAL/RAW/FAST (Field.io). Campaign photo and video, kinetic type and procedural animation composed in real time onto multi-screen clusters from a large content database. Localised per store area. Plugs into Adidas's existing Scala CMS so seasonal content is uploaded through familiar channels; deployable from plug-and-play to bespoke flagship. — [Field.io](https://field.io/work/real-raw-fast-identity-system)
- 113 Spring (Field.io). Biophilic language built on colour, movement and light as the three parameters; a generative portrait system for community members; described as a "living identity". No tool details published on the page. — [Field.io](https://field.io/work/113-spring-generative-brand-expressions)
- Field.io also lists a Meta brand system (four-year partnership with Meta Creative X) and a "Scalable Storytelling" exploration. Not fetched. — [Field.io Meta](https://field.io/work/meta-brand-system), [Scalable Storytelling](https://field.io/explorations/scalable-storytelling)
- OFFF (Collins). Concept: magnetism (attraction/projection). Logo O acts as gravitational core pulling the Fs. Motion language "plays with delay, friction and acceleration"; timing tuned so it is neither too fast (loses pull) nor too slow. Custom typeface OFFF Baikal with Newglyph. — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/collins-transforms-offf-into-a-magnetic-force-with-an-identity-built-on-physics-and-creativity)
- Muse Group (Collins, 2022). Inspired by spectrograms, oscillator waveforms, experimental notation, Labanotation, Suprematist colour. "Emotive visual modules that pulse and harmonise like notes on a staff". "Flexible consistency" across four apps (Ultimate Guitar, MuseScore, Audacity, MuseClass) with shared visual genetics. — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/collins-muse-group-graphic-design-project-220725)
- Instagram motion identity (Studio Dumbar/DEPT, Rose Pilkington): gradient icon with physics-based animated colour shifts and shape morphing. League of Legends MSI (Studio Dumbar/DEPT for Riot): rhythmic layered geometric motion, delivered as a "cookbook" of templates allowing controlled variation. — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/forward-thinking-motion-design-morphing-the-next-era-graphic-design-150125)
- MachineX (Koto, 2026): retro-futurist "bustling ecosystem" world; mascot Dexter shown as full avatar and multicolour technical outline on black; vivid primaries against deep neutrals for high-contrast screens; motion and illustration as repeatable language. — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/koto-machinex-design-identity-star-wars/)
- Squarespace rebrand cited by Mitch Paone (DIA) as Swiss typographic rigour plus procedural logic, durable eight years. — [Paone Substack](https://mitchpaone.substack.com/p/time-is-the-material-from-motion)
- Studio Feixen (Felix Pfäffli) experimented early with generative design and animated posters; site shows bold colour and playful type. No specific system detail found. — [Communication Arts](https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/studio-feixen)
- Wolff Olins named (2018 It's Nice That piece) for Oi (asset morphing around a customer's voice), Qatar Museums (visitors play with geometry), Genesis (pattern generator reflecting mindset); Pentagram for Mellon Foundation shape-shifting M. Snippet-level only. — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/wolff-olins-chris-moody-intelligent-identity-graphic-design-090518)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern across sources: the recognisable "look" is carried by one or two named parameters tied to a concept (magnetism, superposition, biophilic light), not by a filter stack.
- For fxlab: image treatments (dither, lo-fi, grain, gradient blur) and physics-flavoured motion timing (delay/friction/acceleration) map directly onto existing building blocks; Figma Config 2026 is the closest analogue to what fxlab could reproduce as a preset.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Locomotive, ManvsMachine, Buck, Mother Design, Order, Spin, Ordinary Folk, Hello Monday, Bureau Borsche, Superunion, Pentagram/Wolff Olins 2018-2026 detail. Search results did not return usable case-study pages; needs a second pass with direct studio URLs.
- No pre-2018 canonical examples (e.g. MIT Media Lab, Nordkyn/Bruce Mau) were sourced.

## Which recurring visual-language families exist?

### Takeaway
Sources only directly evidence a few families; the taxonomy below is largely my synthesis and needs validating with more examples.

### Cited Findings
- Grain, noise, blur and animated gradients as a 2025-2026 trend; "imperfect by design" hand-drawn and grain textures paired with kinetic systems. — [Three Rooms](https://www.threerooms.com/blog/8-design-trends-shaping-brand-identity-in-2026) (trend blog, low authority)
- Halftone is a tag on Brand New with many entries (not reviewed). — [Brand New: halftone](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/tag/halftone)
- Dither/lo-fi treatment: Figma Config 2026 (above).
- Fluid gradient / physics: Instagram (Dumbar/DEPT), above.
- Data/parameter-driven generative: IBM Quantum, Adidas, 113 Spring (Field.io), above.
- Physics-concept motion: OFFF (Collins), above.
- Kinetic collage: Figma Config 2026 motion "collage-y", above.

### Inferences (my proposed families, with only the sourced examples attached)
1. Dithered / lo-fi digital: Figma Config 2026.
2. Fluid gradient and grain: Instagram motion identity (Dumbar/DEPT).
3. Parametric generative (data or concept as input): IBM Quantum, 113 Spring, Muse Group.
4. Physics-timed motion: OFFF (Collins).
5. Kinetic collage of photo, video, type: Adidas REAL/RAW/FAST, Figma Config.
6. Illustrated world with characters: MachineX (Koto). Mostly out of fxlab scope.

### Gaps
- No sourced examples yet for gritty photocopy/riso/halftone, CRT/scanner artefacts, glassy 3D/soft-focus, liquid distortion. Need searches on named projects (Brand New halftone tag entries would be a start).

## How do studios define and hand over the system?

### Takeaway
Handover ranges from template "cookbooks" to CMS-integrated generators and browser tools. Field.io's approach (named parameters exposed in a tool, integrated with client workflow) is the clearest precedent for fxlab's "turn a language into a tool".

### Cited Findings
- Cookbook of deliverables/templates for controlled variation — Studio Dumbar/DEPT on MSI, [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/forward-thinking-motion-design-morphing-the-next-era-graphic-design-150125)
- Custom browser-based systems let brands generate real-time motion assets independently, citing Spotify Wrapped and Canvas — Connor Campbell, [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/insight/how-to-meaningfully-incorporate-motion-into-branding-with-studio-dumbar-dia-and-connor-campbell)
- Field.io tools: IBM Quantum generative brand tool; Adidas custom tool feeding Scala CMS (links above).
- Nahuel Gerth (creative coder) describes freelance generative tools such as the Iso Type Generator for Future Campus Ruhr; three phases (exploration, refinement, testing), rough budget EUR 10-30k, 2-6 months. Self-reported by the seller. — [Nahuel Gerth](https://nahuelgerth.de/thoughts/brands-in-motion-the-future-of-generative-tools)
- Frontify's modular motion toolkit (by Buff Motion) is presented as scaling identity across teams. Vendor page. — [Buff Motion](https://www.buffmotion.com/projects/frontify-motion-toolkit)
- Figma built its own treatment tools (dither, lo-fi effects) in Figma Make for Config 2026 (above).

### Inferences
- Handover tiers: guidelines/cookbook, then AE/Rive/Lottie kits, then bespoke generator. fxlab sits in the last tier at library scale.
- Naming: parameters are named for the concept (superposition), not the effect (blur amount). fxlab presets could do likewise.

### Gaps
- No public example verified of Rive/Lottie/After Effects template handover, Frontify/Brandpad guideline pages, or "moves" vocabulary. Not found in this pass.

## Best practices and pitfalls for ownable but flexible languages

### Takeaway
Sources agree motion must be designed with the identity from the start, and that flexibility should come from few, meaningful parameters. Detailed seed/range-limit guidance was not found in sources.

### Cited Findings
- Motion added late fails: "Hiring animators to animate design does not work" (Mitch Paone, DIA); "motion should be meaningful" (Dumbar/DEPT). — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/insight/how-to-meaningfully-incorporate-motion-into-branding-with-studio-dumbar-dia-and-connor-campbell)
- Brand and motion industries have incompatible workflows; integration needs people fluent in both. — [Paone Substack](https://mitchpaone.substack.com/p/time-is-the-material-from-motion)
- Timing of a motion rule must be tuned (OFFF: too fast loses the pull, too slow dissipates), i.e. range limits matter. — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/collins-transforms-offf-into-a-magnetic-force-with-an-identity-built-on-physics-and-creativity)
- "Flexible consistency" (Muse Group) and "coherence while flexing" (113 Spring) are the stated goals. Links above.
- AI-generated visual solutions often adhere poorly to design principles (academic paper snippet). — [IASDR](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1472&context=iasdr)

### Inferences
- A consistent treatment applied to all imagery (Figma's dither on every headshot) is a cheap way to make user-supplied content feel owned.
- Anchoring to one physical or conceptual metaphor gives natural parameter limits.

### Gaps
- No sourced guidance on seeds, parametric range limits, or "one signature move" beyond the above; this is my inference. Failure cases (generic gradient-grain sameness) are not documented in fetched sources.
